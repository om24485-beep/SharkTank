The Pitch Doctor

What to change before you pitch a real investor.

Fundable at ₹30 crore, not ₹75 crore. The founder story and the human-in-the-loop design won the room; concentration risk and a founder-only sales engine cost the valuation.
1

Lead with the human-in-the-loop design

Why: The RBI accountability answer flipped the toughest shark. It came in Round 2; it should be on slide 3.

How: Add one line under the solution slide: 'We never approve a loan. Every memo is signed by a human and carries a full audit trail.'

2

Kill the 41% concentration before the next raise

Why: Two sharks named it as the number that keeps them up. It is the single biggest discount on your valuation.

How: Close the two signed January pilots and one new lender. Target: top customer under 30% of ARR within 12 months.

3

Replace the market comp with an earned valuation

Why: 31x ARR from 'other rounds' read as fantasy. Founders who explain their number keep more equity.

How: Show net revenue retention, payback per lender and a 12-month ARR plan with named pipeline. Price off next-year ARR, not peers.

4

Turn sales into a machine, not a founder

Why: A 4-6 month cycle with one seller pushed the brand shark out.

How: Productise a 30-day paid pilot with a fixed success metric (TAT and analyst hours saved), and hire one enterprise seller from an LOS vendor.

5

Name your moat as data, with a number

Why: 'They are slow' did not convince the competition hawk. '11,400 labelled credit files' did.

How: Put the labelled-file count, its growth rate and the fraud-catch rate versus manual review on the competition slide.
