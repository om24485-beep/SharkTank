# Research notes: how the sharks were built

Each shark in this project is an **original fictional character**. To make the panel feel like the real show, each character's investing lens, deal style, red flags and table dynamics were shaped by public reporting about a real Shark Tank India or Shark Tank USA investor. The characters do not carry those investors' names, faces, companies or quotes, and they do not speak for them.

Research was done in November 2026 from news coverage, interviews and data write-ups. Summaries below are paraphrased.

## Show mechanics borrowed
- Five sharks per episode in India; questions overlap, sharks co-invest and add conditions tied to revenue targets ([Unicommerce](https://unicommerce.com/blog/shark-tank-india-judges-brand-investments/)).
- Deals made in the Tank are a starting point; completion after due diligence can take months ([afaqs](https://afaqs.com/news/Television/anupam-mittal-responds-to-shark-tank-delayed-funding-allegations)).
- Royalty versus equity fights, bidding wars, and "I'm out" as the hardest words on the show ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/01/06/shark-tank-cuban-and-corcoran-talk-about-selling-their-businesses.html)).

## Why fictional characters
Simulated dialogue put in a real investor's mouth would be fabricated quotes from a real person. Archetypes keep the realism (the lens, the deal style, the table chemistry) without pretending to be anyone. Add your own shark by editing `../panels.json` and running `npm run agents`.
